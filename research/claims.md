# claim registry

This registry extracts the load-bearing claims of the provisional manifesto and
turns them into objects that can be tested, clarified, revised, or abandoned.
It is a falsification and revision ledger, not a list of settled conclusions.

The registry records claims at the level at which evidence could change them.
It therefore separates historical interpretations, conceptual proposals,
causal hypotheses, empirical generalisations, and normative commitments. A
failure of one claim does not automatically refute every claim in the project;
the dependencies below show which other claims would need reconsideration.

## how to read and update the registry

### claim types

- **Historical interpretation:** a claim about a source, tradition, event, or
  sequence. It requires primary or authoritative historical evidence.
- **Conceptual:** a proposed distinction, definition, or relation. It is judged
  by clarity, consistency, usefulness, and whether it permits discriminating
  observations; it is not falsified in the same way as a causal claim.
- **Causal:** a claim that a specified mechanism changes an outcome under stated
  conditions.
- **Empirical generalisation:** a recurring or directional pattern across cases.
- **Model claim:** a claim that a formal representation earns its complexity by
  distinguishing outcomes or outperforming a simpler representation.
- **Normative:** an explicit evaluative commitment. Evidence can expose factual
  premises or consequences, but cannot by itself supply the value judgement.

Some entries have more than one type. The first type names the main burden of
proof.

### confidence

Confidence refers to the current warrant for the claim as written, not its
importance or intuitive appeal.

| Level | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **C0 — unassessed** | Extracted but not yet investigated. |
| **C1 — conjectural** | A plausible research hypothesis with little or no assembled evidence. |
| **C2 — provisional** | Some relevant evidence or established theory supports it, but rivals and negative cases remain open. |
| **C3 — supported** | Multiple appropriate sources or tests support it across a declared domain. |
| **C4 — robust** | It has survived strong rival explanations, negative cases, and sensitivity or replication tests within that domain. |

No manifesto claim currently exceeds C1. The project has not yet assembled the
evidence required for a higher rating.

### status

- **Proposed:** retained in its present form as a target for investigation.
- **Needs specification:** directionally usable, but missing a domain, scale,
  measure, mechanism, or comparison.
- **Needs revision:** the present wording conflates categories, overreaches, or
  has already met contrary analysis.
- **Under test:** a research design or model is actively evaluating it.
- **Provisionally retained:** it has survived a recorded test within a stated
  domain.
- **Rejected:** a recorded decision has abandoned it in its present form.

Evidence entries must link to a source note, case record, dataset, model result,
or decision record. Absence of recorded evidence is written as **none
assembled**, not silently treated as support. Confidence changes and wording
changes require a dated revision note.

## claims

### CM-01 — antinomies as mutually dependent relations

- **Type:** Conceptual; causal.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Needs specification.
- **Statement:** Some opposed social tendencies are co-constitutive: each helps
  produce, enable, or delimit the other within a shared relation, so removing
  either pole transforms the relation rather than finally resolving it.
- **Source:** [Manifesto introduction](../MANIFESTO.md#cybernetic-mutualism) and
  [section 2](../MANIFESTO.md#2-antinomies-do-not-end).
- **Unit and scope:** A named pair of tendencies within a specified institution,
  network, or social process over a declared interval. The claim does not apply
  to every opposition.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Reciprocal positive and negative feedback, constraint,
  and dependence reproduce both poles while changing their relative strength.
- **Observable implication:** Interventions that suppress one pole change the
  production or behaviour of the other; observations show directed effects in
  both directions rather than a single common cause alone.
- **Rivals:** Independent responses to a third variable; a temporary trade-off;
  a logical contradiction with no causal relation; unilateral production by
  one pole.
- **Could weaken it:** Candidate cases repeatedly show one-way dependence,
  removable poles with an unchanged relation, or no discriminating observation
  between antinomy and ordinary interaction.
- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — none assembled.
- **Dependencies:** Grounds CM-04, CM-05, CM-12, and CM-13.
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — extracted; wording narrowed from all
  manifesto oppositions to **some** named social tendencies.

### CM-02 — mutualism as a theory of progress

- **Type:** Historical interpretation.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Needs specification.
- **Statement:** A defensible strand of Proudhon's mutualism treats progress,
  serial transformation, collective force, and contradiction as more basic
  concerns than a collection of economic proposals alone.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 1](../MANIFESTO.md#1-mutualism-is-a-theory-of-progress).
- **Unit and scope:** Named works and periods in Proudhon's corpus, compared
  with later mutualist interpretations; it is not yet a claim about all
  mutualists.
- **Proposed test:** Establish meanings and changes across primary texts, then
  distinguish textual attribution from the project's cybernetic
  reconstruction.
- **Observable implication:** Primary texts make historical development and
  seriality necessary to the economic proposals rather than merely adjacent
  themes.
- **Rivals:** Progress is peripheral; the economic proposals are analytically
  independent; the apparent theory is a retrospective reconstruction.
- **Could weaken it:** Chronological close reading shows no stable developmental
  account or shows that the claimed unity depends on later terminology.
- **Evidence:** For — the glossary lists candidate primary sources but does not
  complete the close reading. Against — none assembled.
- **Dependencies:** Affects the project's genealogy, but failure would not by
  itself refute the communication or control hypotheses.
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — extracted and restricted to a defensible
  strand rather than mutualism without qualification.

### CM-03 — historical change is nonlinear and scale-dependent

- **Type:** Empirical generalisation.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Needs specification.
- **Statement:** Measures of historical change can reverse direction across
  time, scale, domain, or population; gains in one declared capacity can
  coexist with losses in another.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 1](../MANIFESTO.md#1-mutualism-is-a-theory-of-progress).
- **Unit and scope:** A named capacity, population, spatial scale, and interval
  across historical transitions.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Coupled processes have different delays, thresholds,
  and resource constraints, so an intervention can produce divergent local and
  system-level effects.
- **Observable implication:** Multiscale measures of the same transition show
  heterogeneous trajectories that an aggregate linear measure conceals.
- **Rivals:** Stable monotonic development; apparent reversals produced only by
  changing definitions, case selection, or measurement error.
- **Could weaken it:** Prespecified multiscale comparisons add no explanatory
  or predictive value over a single trend, or the claimed reversals disappear
  under stable measurement.
- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — none assembled.
- **Dependencies:** Qualifies CM-20 and prevents descriptive direction from
  being treated as universal moral progress.
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — extracted.

### CM-04 — cybernetic dynamics can formalise antinomies

- **Type:** Model claim.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Proposed.
- **Statement:** A minimal dynamical model using feedback sign, delay,
  saturation, and adaptation can distinguish meanings of antinomic balance by
  producing equilibrium, oscillation, lock-in, bifurcation, or collapse.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 2](../MANIFESTO.md#2-antinomies-do-not-end).
- **Unit and scope:** Initially, a two-variable model of a single specified
  antinomy; later, empirically parameterised cases.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Different coupling functions and parameter regions
  generate qualitatively distinct regimes.
- **Observable implication:** The model yields behaviours or conditional
  predictions that prose and an uncoupled baseline do not, and those behaviours
  remain under sensitivity analysis.
- **Rivals:** The formalisation merely renames the prose; an uncoupled or
  common-shock model explains the same regimes with fewer assumptions.
- **Could weaken it:** No mapping from variables to observations, no new
  distinction, extreme parameter fragility, or equal performance by a simpler
  model.
- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — none assembled.
- **Dependencies:** Requires a specified instance of CM-01.
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — extracted as a model-performance claim,
  not evidence that Proudhon's language was already cybernetic.

### CM-05 — no social order ends antagonism

- **Type:** Empirical generalisation.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Needs revision.
- **Statement:** The manifesto's universal assertion that there is no final
  decentralisation, plan, class, market, or revolution should be replaced by a
  bounded hypothesis: durable social orders tend to generate new asymmetries,
  conflicts, and counterforces under identifiable conditions.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 2](../MANIFESTO.md#2-antinomies-do-not-end).
- **Unit and scope:** Institutional orders observed over a period long enough
  for distributional and organisational after-effects to appear.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Differentiated positions, accumulated advantages,
  changing environments, and responses to prior controls generate new
  asymmetries and conflicts.
- **Observable implication:** Orders that suppress one conflict develop new
  measurable concentrations, exclusions, or organised counterforces more often
  than matched cases without the proposed conditions.
- **Rivals:** Conflict is caused by contingent external shocks; some stable
  arrangements remove the relevant asymmetry without functional replacement.
- **Could weaken it:** Long-duration cases retain low asymmetry and no organised
  counterforce, or the mechanism cannot distinguish recurrence from the trivial
  statement that change continues.
- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — none assembled.
- **Dependencies:** Draws on CM-01 but is a separate historical
  generalisation.
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — marked **needs revision** because the
  manifesto's absolute form is not falsifiable and groups unlike institutions.

### CM-06 — society depends on reproduced communication

- **Type:** Conceptual; causal.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Needs specification.
- **Statement:** The reproduction of recognisably human social organisation
  requires communication relations that persist or recur across interactions.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 3](../MANIFESTO.md#3-communication-is-material).
- **Unit and scope:** Human groups and institutions over intervals longer than a
  single encounter.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Signals allow expectations, roles, memory,
  coordination, and sanctions to be reproduced across persons and time.
- **Observable implication:** When relevant channels are disrupted beyond the
  redundancy of a group, coordinated roles and institutional continuity decay
  in predictable ways.
- **Rivals:** Material co-presence or coercion alone sustains the same social
  organisation; communication is definitional rather than causally informative.
- **Could weaken it:** The term is defined so broadly that every interaction
  counts, or disruptions produce no discriminating effects beyond other lost
  resources.
- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — none assembled.
- **Dependencies:** Motivates CM-07 but does not establish topology's priority.
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — extracted and narrowed from societies
  "exist only" to reproduction of social organisation.

### CM-07 — communication topology constrains social organisation

- **Type:** Causal.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Needs specification.
- **Statement:** Holding relevant resources and actors sufficiently comparable,
  differences in who can communicate with whom, through which paths and at what
  cost, change which forms of coordination are feasible and sustainable.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 3](../MANIFESTO.md#3-communication-is-material).
- **Unit and scope:** A declared communication-network layer and a specified
  organisational outcome.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Reach, path redundancy, latency, fidelity,
  addressability, and return capacity alter coordination cost, observability,
  enforcement, and diffusion.
- **Observable implication:** Exogenous or independently dated topology changes
  precede changes in specified organisational forms; identical agent rules
  produce different feasible forms under changed graph constraints.
- **Rivals:** Production, ownership, state capacity, ideology, ecology, or
  demography causes both network and organisation; observed traffic rather than
  feasible topology does the work.
- **Could weaken it:** Topology measures add no out-of-sample or comparative
  explanatory power, or predicted infeasible organisations persist without
  hidden channels.
- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — none assembled.
- **Dependencies:** Requires the operational definitions in
  [the glossary](glossary.md#topology).
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — extracted.

### CM-08 — communication topology can be causally prior

- **Type:** Causal.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Needs specification.
- **Statement:** In at least some historical transitions, a change in
  communication substrate or topology is a necessary earlier constraint on a
  change in productive or political organisation, and explains its timing or
  variation better than a production-first account.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 3](../MANIFESTO.md#3-communication-is-material).
- **Unit and scope:** Individually reconstructed transitions with dated changes
  in topology, production, ownership, state organisation, and relevant
  confounders.
- **Proposed mechanism:** A topology change alters feasible coordination before
  actors select and stabilise a new institution within that space.
- **Observable implication:** Process tracing finds the network change before
  the institutional outcome, failed diffusion where the constraint is absent,
  and variation matching the topology rather than its strongest rival.
- **Rivals:** Production-first, state-first, ecological, demographic, military,
  or common-cause explanations; institutions may finance and select the network
  first.
- **Could weaken it:** Production or political change consistently precedes and
  predicts topology better, or the proposed ordering reverses after improved
  dating and source criticism.
- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — the manifesto itself notes that
  productive and scientific arrangements can generate communication
  technologies; no cases have yet adjudicated direction.
- **Dependencies:** Stronger than CM-07; CM-07 could survive this claim's
  rejection.
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — narrowed from general causal priority to
  priority in at least some reconstructable transitions.

### CM-09 — durable phenomena enter a five-domain circuit

- **Type:** Causal; model claim.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Needs specification.
- **Statement:** For some durable social phenomena, a coupled model of
  communication topology (`T`), production (`P`), artefacts (`A`), worldview
  (`W`), and political organisation (`S`) explains timing, variation, or regime
  change better than a simpler model that omits one or more domains.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 4](../MANIFESTO.md#4-society-is-a-recursive-circuit).
- **Unit and scope:** A specified phenomenon whose observations can be assigned
  to declared domains and time steps; not every event must occupy all domains.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Asymmetric cross-domain coupling, delay, threshold,
  and feedback transmit and transform disturbances.
- **Observable implication:** Five-domain case narratives or models improve
  discriminating explanation or prediction over preregistered reduced
  baselines; ablation identifies consequential couplings.
- **Rivals:** Independent shocks; a two-domain production/state model; domain
  labels redescribe the same variable; unrestricted feedback overfits any
  history.
- **Could weaken it:** Domains cannot be assigned reliably, omitted-domain
  models perform as well, or flexible couplings explain every possible result.
- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — none assembled.
- **Dependencies:** Does not require CM-08; causal priority can differ by case.
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — narrowed from "most durable human
  phenomena" to a comparative model claim about some specified phenomena.

### CM-10 — substrates define feasible topology spaces

- **Type:** Conceptual; causal.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Needs revision.
- **Statement:** A communication substrate's material capacity-and-cost profile
  excludes some graph structures and makes others feasible under stated
  resource conditions; institutions and ownership regimes select paths within
  that constrained space.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 5](../MANIFESTO.md#5-substrates-define-spaces-phases-trace-paths).
- **Unit and scope:** A declared substrate, resource envelope, graph layer, and
  threshold for practical feasibility.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Limits on reach, latency, copying, durability,
  addressability, verification, and return capacity bound possible edges and
  network operations.
- **Observable implication:** Independently measured capacities rule out
  specified structures or make their maintenance costs sharply different;
  institutions vary within rather than outside those bounds.
- **Rivals:** Political permission, ownership, demand, or state capacity alone
  defines the observed space; substrate categories add no constraint.
- **Could weaken it:** Supposedly excluded structures appear at ordinary cost,
  paired substrates permit indistinguishable spaces, or `Ω` cannot be measured
  without defining it from observed institutions.
- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — conceptual review in
  [the glossary](glossary.md#substrate) finds that the manifesto's sequence mixes
  substrates, institutional regimes, and hypothetical capacity profiles.
- **Dependencies:** Supports CM-11 and supplies a mechanism for CM-07.
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — marked **needs revision**. "Platform
  capture" should move from the substrate sequence to an institutional or
  ownership layer; "persistent, global, live interconnection" remains a
  hypothesised capacity profile, not an established substrate.

### CM-11 — substrate change pressures inherited institutions

- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.

- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — none assembled.

### CM-12 — decentralisation has generative and disorganising effects

- **Type:** Causal.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Needs specification.
- **Statement:** Under specifiable conditions, decreasing concentration in a
  named network layer increases variation, local information, experimentation,
  or edge formation while also raising coordination cost, fragmentation, or
  exposure to aggregators.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 6](../MANIFESTO.md#6-centralisation-and-decentralisation-mutually-produce-one-another).
- **Unit and scope:** Before-and-after distributions in one declared layer,
  holding the aggregation level and observation window stable.
- **Proposed mechanism:** More independent decision sites diversify trials and
  information while reducing common knowledge, standards, and bargaining power
  against scale-seeking intermediaries.
- **Observable implication:** Outcomes vary non-monotonically with measured
  concentration and with interoperability, exit cost, and economies of scale.
- **Rivals:** The listed outcomes come from resources, group size, or ideology;
  decentralisation is only an institutional label.
- **Could weaken it:** Stable network measures show no conditional relation, or
  effects disappear when resources and selection into structure are addressed.
- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — none assembled.
- **Dependencies:** Uses [the glossary definition](glossary.md#decentralisation)
  and must not infer one layer from another.
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — extracted.

### CM-14 — decentralising innovations tend toward recapture

- **Type:** Empirical generalisation; causal.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Needs specification.
- **Statement:** Where preferential attachment, scale economies, or control of
  standards are strong and exit or interoperability are weak, a
  decentralising innovation tends to generate new hubs and renewed
  concentration in at least one consequential layer.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 6](../MANIFESTO.md#6-centralisation-and-decentralisation-mutually-produce-one-another).
- **Unit and scope:** Network transitions with layer-specific concentration
  measured from introduction through consolidation.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Successful nodes accumulate links and data; shared
  standards reduce switching; aggregation advantages turn reach into control
  over discovery, routing, or exchange.
- **Observable implication:** Recapture is more frequent or faster where scale
  incentives and switching costs are stronger, and is attenuated by effective
  interoperability, congestion, antitrust, federation, or exit.
- **Rivals:** Concentration is imposed by the state or ownership before the
  innovation; observed hubs are efficient but contestable; cycles are an
  artefact of changing layer or aggregation level.
- **Could weaken it:** Recapture does not occur in high-incentive cases, occurs
  equally without the proposed mechanisms, or vanishes under stable
  layer-specific measures.
- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — none assembled.
- **Dependencies:** A testable specialisation of CM-12 and CM-13.
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — narrowed from an expected universal
  historical pattern to a conditional hypothesis.

### CM-15 — inhibitory institutions can make rivalry productive

- **Type:** Causal.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Needs specification.
- **Statement:** Rivalry yields sustained information or innovation without
  monopoly, arms race, exhaustion, or collapse only within ranges shaped by
  inhibitory institutions such as resource ceilings, transparency, exit,
  reciprocal guarantee, federation, and sanctions against capture.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 7](../MANIFESTO.md#7-conflict-can-be-productive-without-being-good).
- **Unit and scope:** A specified population of rivals, output measure, harm
  measure, resource environment, and institutional rule set.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Contest supplies search and error signals; nonlinear
  escalation creates damage; negative feedback limits escalation while
  preserving variation.
- **Observable implication:** Output and diversity peak within bounded regions
  of rivalry and inhibition, while removing or overwhelming the inhibitors
  produces concentration or collapse.
- **Rivals:** Cooperation or resources alone cause innovation; inhibitors merely
  suppress output; selection effects put successful fields under these rules.
- **Could weaken it:** The proposed interventions do not preserve output and
  diversity, harms remain linear or absent, or simpler resource explanations
  account for the results.
- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — none assembled.
- **Dependencies:** May instantiate CM-01 but can be tested independently as an
  institutional hypothesis.
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — extracted.

### CM-16 — recurrence does not confer legitimacy

- **Type:** Conceptual; normative.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Proposed.
- **Statement:** Evidence that hierarchy, status difference, conflict, or
  domination recurs cannot by itself justify that relation; political
  legitimacy requires an explicit normative argument independent of observed
  frequency or stability.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 7](../MANIFESTO.md#7-conflict-can-be-productive-without-being-good).
- **Unit and scope:** Inferences from descriptive or causal findings to
  normative conclusions.
- **Proposed test:** Audit project outputs for unmarked movement from "recurs,"
  "stabilises," or "is functional" to "is right" or "ought to continue."
- **Observable implication:** Descriptively identical evidence remains
  compatible with different moral evaluations unless an additional value
  premise is stated.
- **Rivals:** Naturalistic, functionalist, or stability-based accounts that
  explicitly defend recurrence as a source of authority.
- **Could weaken it:** A successful argument establishes why the relevant
  descriptive property entails authority without an independent normative
  premise; empirical recurrence alone cannot perform this task.
- **Evidence:** For — a logical distinction, not yet a completed philosophical
  argument. Against — none assembled.
- **Dependencies:** Constrains interpretation of CM-05, CM-12 through CM-15,
  CM-19, and CM-24.
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — extracted as a normative guardrail rather
  than an empirical prediction.

### CM-17 — class struggle is a central historical motor

- **Type:** Historical interpretation; causal.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Needs specification.
- **Statement:** Conflicts organised around control of production, surplus,
  labour, and class position explain consequential historical changes in a
  substantial but bounded domain of cases.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 8](../MANIFESTO.md#8-marx-is-necessary-and-insufficient).
- **Unit and scope:** Must be set per case; "central motor of history" is too
  broad until compared with state, geopolitical, ecological, demographic, and
  communication mechanisms.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Class position shapes interests, organisational
  capacity, appropriation, conflict, and institutional transformation.
- **Observable implication:** Class-structured variables explain timing and
  outcomes after rival mechanisms are specified, and intervention in class
  relations changes the predicted process.
- **Rivals:** Elite competition, state autonomy, nationalism, ecology,
  demography, or communication structure explains the same changes better.
- **Could weaken it:** Class categories cannot be operationalised consistently,
  add little explanatory power, or are downstream of stronger mechanisms in the
  selected domain.
- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — none assembled.
- **Dependencies:** In tension, but not logical conflict, with CM-18.
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — marked **needs specification** because the
  manifesto gives no unit, comparison class, or threshold for "central."

### CM-18 — communication sometimes explains production better than the reverse

- **Type:** Causal.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Needs specification.
- **Statement:** In some cases, communication topology conditions which
  productive relations can be coordinated, observed, enforced, and imagined,
  and a communication-first timeline explains the transition better than a
  production-first timeline.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 8](../MANIFESTO.md#8-marx-is-necessary-and-insufficient).
- **Unit and scope:** Paired case timelines with independently dated changes in
  networks, ownership, production, and political enforcement.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Observability, addressability, latency, and routing
  determine the scale and form at which labour, exchange, accounting, and
  command can operate.
- **Observable implication:** Productive forms fail or remain local without the
  relevant communication capacities and diffuse after those capacities appear,
  conditional on resources and authority.
- **Rivals:** Productive demand finances the channel; ownership and state power
  configure both production and topology; the order is reciprocal with no
  stable priority.
- **Could weaken it:** Production-first timelines consistently fit timing,
  failed diffusion, and geographic variation better, or communication measures
  contribute no independent constraint.
- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — none assembled.
- **Dependencies:** Applies CM-08 specifically to production relations.
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — extracted.

### CM-19 — anti-capitalist orders can reproduce class and accumulation

- **Type:** Empirical generalisation.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Needs specification.
- **Statement:** Removing private capitalist ownership or establishing
  anti-capitalist institutions is not sufficient to prevent new elite
  formation, class-like stratification, or accelerated accumulation.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 8](../MANIFESTO.md#8-marx-is-necessary-and-insufficient).
- **Unit and scope:** Named revolutionary or anti-capitalist institutions, with
  explicit measures for ownership, control, surplus allocation, elite
  reproduction, and accumulation.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Control of political appointment, information,
  allocation, and organisational position supplies alternative bases for
  durable advantage.
- **Observable implication:** Stratification or accumulation persists or
  reappears where private title is curtailed but these control positions remain
  concentrated.
- **Rivals:** Observed elites are transitional, occupational, or externally
  imposed rather than class-like; capitalist world-system pressure is the
  sufficient cause.
- **Could weaken it:** Stable cases remove private ownership and the proposed
  alternative mechanisms without reproducing measurable durable advantage, or
  the concepts are stretched until every differentiation counts as class.
- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — none assembled.
- **Dependencies:** Relevant to CM-05 and CM-23 but does not establish either.
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — combined several manifesto examples into a
  sufficiency claim with explicit measurement requirements.

### CM-20 — communication capacities have a long-run direction

- **Type:** Empirical generalisation.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Needs specification.
- **Statement:** Across a prespecified historical sample and interval, at least
  some communication capacities—extent, persistence, speed, differentiation,
  or declared complexity dimensions—show a positive long-run tendency despite
  local stagnation and reversal.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 9](../MANIFESTO.md#9-direction-does-not-require-a-destination).
- **Unit and scope:** Each capacity requires its own proxy, uncertainty range,
  temporal and geographic coverage, and rule for aggregation; the claim is not
  a single complexity index.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Selection retains some systems with greater reach or
  coordination capacity when their energetic and maintenance conditions can be
  met; collapse can reverse the tendency.
- **Observable implication:** Preregistered proxies distinguish direction from
  stagnation, reversal, and survivorship bias and remain positive under
  reasonable sampling and uncertainty choices.
- **Rivals:** Archival and survivor bias; population growth alone; cyclical or
  regionally bounded change; complexity costs select for simplification.
- **Could weaken it:** Trends vanish or reverse with negative cases, missing-data
  correction, stable definitions, energetic costs, or alternative start dates.
- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — none assembled.
- **Dependencies:** Must remain separate from normative progress under CM-03
  and [the glossary](glossary.md#progress).
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — disaggregated the manifesto's singular
  tendency into separately testable capacities.

### CM-21 — models can reflexively change their object

- **Type:** Causal.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Proposed.
- **Statement:** When a public model changes actors' expectations or
  incentives, its use can alter the behaviour and system it describes,
  including by defeating or amplifying its initial prediction.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 10](../MANIFESTO.md#10-no-free-will-is-required).
- **Unit and scope:** Public forecasts, classifications, targets, or theories
  whose audiences can observe or infer them and can affect the modelled outcome.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Model output changes belief; belief changes action;
  aggregated action changes the target process or data-generating mechanism.
- **Observable implication:** Otherwise comparable actors exposed to a model
  change behaviour, and forecast performance changes after publication or use.
- **Rivals:** Behaviour changes because of simultaneous intervention,
  selection, or new information rather than reflexive uptake.
- **Could weaken it:** Exposure and uptake have no measurable behavioural
  pathway, or outcome changes precede the model's circulation.
- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — none assembled.
- **Dependencies:** Supports second-order modelling but is independent of a
  metaphysical position on free will.
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — extracted.

### CM-22 — determinism permits conditional explanation

- **Type:** Conceptual; model claim.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Needs revision.
- **Statement:** The project's useful methodological commitment is to
  conditional causal explanation under bounded information, nonlinear dynamics,
  noise, and reflexivity; this commitment does not require resolving
  metaphysical determinism or proving exact prediction impossible in every
  system.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 10](../MANIFESTO.md#10-no-free-will-is-required).
- **Unit and scope:** Explanatory models of social regimes under declared
  parameters, uncertainty, and observation limits.
- **Proposed test:** Compare conditional regime predictions with unconditional
  forecasts and with models that make different causal assumptions.
- **Observable implication:** Models report ranges, sensitivities, and changing
  probabilities rather than deriving certainty from determinism.
- **Rivals:** Metaphysical determinism is indispensable to the empirical
  program; or causal explanation requires uncaused agency.
- **Could weaken it:** The metaphysical premise produces a discriminating
  empirical prediction unavailable to ordinary causal modelling, or the
  conditional programme covertly relies on it.
- **Evidence:** For — methodological consistency with the roadmap. Against — no
  argument has established the manifesto's metaphysical assertions about
  consciousness or universal impossibility of exact prediction.
- **Dependencies:** CM-21 and the empirical programme do not depend on settling
  metaphysical determinism.
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — marked **needs revision** to separate a
  usable methodological stance from stronger metaphysical and universal claims.

### CM-23 — steering institutions are partial internal controllers

- **Type:** Conceptual; causal.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Needs specification.
- **Statement:** States, parties, markets, sciences, media, and bureaucracies

- **Unit and scope:** A named institution, controlled variable or viability
  range, environment, reporting channel, actuation mechanism, and interval.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Internal controllers sample only part of environmental
  variety; delays and actors' responses transform signals; concentrated

- **Observable implication:** Failure modes covary with measurable mismatch in
  variety, latency, distortion, incentives, and audit capacity rather than with
  institutional labels alone.
- **Rivals:** Failures arise from bad objectives, resource shortage, or external
  shocks without an information-control mechanism; the controller metaphor
  adds no discriminating value.
- **Could weaken it:** Control variables and channels cannot be specified, or
  control measures do not improve explanation over conventional institutional
  accounts.
- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — none assembled.

### CM-24 — viable control is likely to be hybrid and recursive

- **Type:** Causal.
- **Confidence:** C1 — conjectural.
- **Status:** Needs specification.
- **Statement:** Under environments with changing variety, delay, strategic
  reporting, and capture risk, combinations of distributed experimentation,
  shared infrastructure, enforceable constraints, and recursive audit can
  maintain declared viability ranges better than a purely central or purely
  decentralised controller.
- **Source:** [Manifesto section 11](../MANIFESTO.md#11-politics-is-a-problem-of-control-without-a-controller).
- **Unit and scope:** A specified disturbance distribution, performance and
  cost function, controller architectures, and political allocation of errors
  and burdens.
- **Proposed mechanism:** Distributed units supply response variety and local
  information; shared infrastructure supplies memory and coordination;
  constraints limit externalities; recursive audit detects model and controller
  failure.
- **Observable implication:** Hybrid controllers dominate only in identifiable
  parameter regions, with trade-offs visible in response quality, latency,
  cost, adaptation, and capture.
- **Rivals:** A central, price-mediated, or fully polycentric architecture
  performs as well with less complexity; apparent benefits come from greater
  resources or favourable objectives.
- **Could weaken it:** Hybrid advantage disappears under equal information and
  resource budgets, or no parameter region distinguishes the architectures.
- **Evidence:** For — none assembled. Against — none assembled.
- **Dependencies:** A constructive hypothesis built from CM-12, CM-13, and
  CM-23; CM-16 prevents performance from being equated with legitimacy.
- **Revision history:** 2026-08-30 — extracted as a conditional comparison, not
  a universal constitutional prescription.

## methodological commitments

These are rules for investigating the claims, not evidence that the claims are
true. They come primarily from [manifesto section
12](../MANIFESTO.md#12-the-theory-must-risk-being-wrong) and the roadmap.

1. Define units, network layers, scales, intervals, and comparison classes.
2. Separate substrate capacities, topology, ownership, protocol authority,
   routing discretion, and observed use.
3. Record supportive, difficult, and negative cases rather than only exemplary
   ones.
4. State rival mechanisms and what result would favour them.
5. Compare coupled models with reduced, independent-shock, and common-cause
   baselines.
6. Publish assumptions, code, reproducible seeds, sensitivity tests, and
   negative results for formal models.
7. Distinguish failure of a measure, dataset, local model, causal claim, and the
   wider programme.
8. Keep descriptive recurrence, functional performance, and normative
   justification separate.

## revision queue exposed by the first extraction

The registry itself has already found claims that should not remain in their
current manifesto form:

1. **The substrate sequence mixes levels.** Per CM-10 and the glossary,
   platform capture is an institutional and ownership regime on digital
   substrates, not a substrate equivalent to speech, writing, print, broadcast,
   or digital networks.
2. **Several universal statements need domains.** "Antinomies do not end," "no
   final class," and "most durable human phenomena" currently resist failure by
   ranging over unspecified cases and times. CM-01, CM-05, and CM-09 supply
   bounded replacements.
3. **Causal priority must be comparative.** CM-08 replaces a general hierarchy
   with case-level rival timelines that can favour communication-first,
   production-first, state-first, or reciprocal explanations.
4. **Metaphysics and method should separate.** CM-22 preserves conditional
   causal explanation without making the research programme depend on an
   untested account of consciousness or a universal statement about prediction.
5. **Historical direction is a vector, not a scalar.** CM-20 requires separate
   proxies and uncertainty for extent, persistence, speed, differentiation, and
   complexity rather than treating them as one ascent.

These are proposed manifesto revisions, not silent edits. Each should receive a
decision record when the manifesto is next revised.
