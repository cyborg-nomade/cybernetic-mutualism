# Print/Reformation Dated Evidence Ledger

## Reading the Records

Collected 2026-09-08 under freeze `fdea00e` and the
[codebook](print-reformation-codebook.md). Source IDs resolve to the
[source audit](print-reformation-source-audit.md#inspected-sources), which supplies
URLs, access extent, standpoint, and proximity. Those source attributes apply
to each citing record. S01, S02 and S09 distinguish contemporary prescription
from modern introduction; other historical descriptions are retrospective.

The interval column gives earliest/latest dates at the stated precision; a
single year is the full calendar year, not 1 January. An exact reported day is
retained without calendar conversion. `unknown` is unobserved, never zero.
Links order the reconstruction; they do not assert causation. The same event
appearing in multiple sources is one observation. Imperial acts are external
inputs to ES/DS, not observations of uniform local compliance.

Outcome components are stated separately from public-reorganization status.
Confidence concerns the recorded act or report, not its historical effects.

## Conclusions and Next Steps

The ledger warrants comparison of specific acts and their sequence, not a complete
adoption panel. It exposes a direct temporal problem for a battle-result trigger
of E11, a clear authorization/enforcement distinction in E13–E17, and political
selection of institutional change in E05–E07. These are usable constraints on
CM-07–CM-09's explanations even while the full durability outcome remains open.

Next, retrieve the original E11 decree and pre-April diplomatic records, and pair
Saxon visitation instructions with dated implementation returns. Those observations
would discriminate mechanisms more directly than adding unlinked edition counts.
