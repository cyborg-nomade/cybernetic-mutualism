# Print, Reformation, and institutional selection: a paired case design

## Status and purpose

This document specifies a comparison before the project assembles or codes the
case evidence. It is a design, not a finding. Its purpose is to test whether the
spread of print helps explain the timing and geography of durable confessional
reorganization, and to determine what print cannot explain without political,
economic, and religious mechanisms.

The positive case is the German-speaking Holy Roman Empire from 1517 to 1555,
with a nested reconstruction of Wittenberg and Electoral Saxony. The difficult
comparison is the Italian peninsula from 1517 to 1565, with a nested
reconstruction of Venice and its mainland dominion. The comparison ends ten
years later in Italy so that the analysis includes the Roman Inquisition, the
1559 Pauline Index, and the close of the Council of Trent without treating
later confessional outcomes as causes of earlier events.

This pairing deliberately selects divergent outcomes. It therefore cannot
estimate how frequently print produced reform across Europe. It can, however,
test a proposed mechanism against a print-rich setting in which reforming and
heterodox ideas circulated without producing a durable public Protestant
church. The nested cases keep the comparison close to identifiable printers,
rulers, magistrates, clergy, and ordinances rather than making Germany and Italy
act as undifferentiated actors.

## Question and claims under test

The central question is: under what conditions did print-mediated
communication relations contribute to durable public confessional
reorganization rather than only to the circulation of criticism, devotion, or
heterodox belief?

The design tests [CM-07](../claims.md#cm-07--communication-topology-constrains-social-organisation),
which claims that communication topology changes feasible forms of
coordination; [CM-08](../claims.md#cm-08--communication-topology-can-be-causally-prior),
which makes the stronger claim that topology can explain the timing or
variation of an institutional transition better than a production-first or
state-first account; [CM-09](../claims.md#cm-09--durable-phenomena-enter-a-five-domain-circuit),
which claims that a coupled five-domain account can outperform a reduced one.

The comparison does not test whether movable-type printing caused the
Reformation in isolation. Print was already established in both regions before
1517. The relevant variation lies in uses and relations built on that
substrate: cheap vernacular formats, reprinting between centers, distribution,
identifiable and anonymous authorship, preaching from printed texts, and the
legal and coercive control of production and possession.

For that reason, the design does not yet put
[CM-11](../claims.md#cm-11--substrate-change-pressures-inherited-institutions)
under test. That claim requires an independently measured change in substrate
capacities, not merely new content, demand, institutions, or traffic on an
established substrate. A later capacity reconstruction may add such a test, but
the present design must not assume its treatment into existence.

## Bounded outcome

The principal outcome is **durable public confessional reorganization** in a
territory or self-governing city. An observation qualifies when a legally or
politically competent authority authorized a new public church order, worship,
or clerical regime and the arrangement persisted for at least ten years or to a
case-ending settlement. The date is the earliest effective act, not the first
private profession of belief or the retrospective date claimed by a later
church.

Evidence after the explanatory window may be used only to establish whether an
arrangement met the ten-year durability rule. Later survival or failure must
not be projected backward as an explanation of adoption.

The component observations are the enactment and implementation of church
ordinances; authorization of public worship and preaching; appointment,
removal, or supervision of clergy; changes in church property or finance; and
continued operation despite imperial, papal, or local opposition. These
components must be recorded separately because a city may tolerate preaching
without reorganizing a church, or enact an ordinance that it cannot enforce.

Three secondary outcomes prevent the public-institutional measure from erasing
unsuccessful or concealed change. **Discursive diffusion** records the
appearance, reprinting, translation, sale, possession, or documented reception
of reforming texts. **Organized dissent** records recurring groups, preachers,
correspondence, conventicles, or protection networks without recognized public
institutions. **Institutional counter-adaptation** records censorship,
licensing, inquisitorial procedure, Catholic preaching and publication, or
other changes by an inherited church or state in response to the same
communications.

Private belief is not a directly measurable outcome. Testimony from trials,
letters, visitation records, and hostile reports can establish reported speech
or conduct, but it cannot be converted mechanically into population belief.

## Why the cases are comparable and difficult

Both regions contained old and new printing centers, commercial cities,
universities or humanist networks, vernacular and Latin readerships, contested
relations between secular and ecclesiastical authority, and multiple political
jurisdictions. Northern Italy was not isolated from reforming ideas, and Venice
was not a low-print residual category. Euan Cameron's survey warns against
treating Italians as inherently unreceptive, while Andrew Pettegree and Matthew
Hall warn that conclusions drawn from evangelical dominance of the German
press do not automatically travel to other regional print cultures.

The cases are not matched on every cause. The imperial constitution, the
position of territorial princes, proximity to Rome, the structure of bishoprics
and religious orders, war, language markets, and Venice's geopolitical
interests differ. These are not defects to be controlled away by assertion.
They are rival mechanisms whose sequence and interaction with print must be
reconstructed.

The nested contrast sharpens the problem. Wittenberg was a small and initially
unpromising print center whose output changed with the Luther–Cranach
collaboration and demand for inexpensive German works. Venice was already a
major European center with extensive commercial connections and abundant cheap
vernacular print. If raw press capacity or urban fragmentation were sufficient,
Venice should not be the negative case. If political protection were sufficient
without print, Electoral Saxony should have been able to institutionalize the
same change without the rapid reproduction and outward circulation of texts.

## Explanatory alternatives and discriminating expectations

### Topology-first account

The topology-first account proposes that lower copying costs, distributed
reprinting, and connections among production centers allowed reformers to reach
audiences and coordinate supporters faster than inherited authorities could
filter them. It expects the relevant change in print relations to precede
public reorganization, early local exposure to predict earlier organization,
and weakly connected places to lag despite comparable grievances. Within
Italy, it expects places with stronger cross-border and reprint connections to
show earlier or more organized dissent even if repression prevents a public
church.

This account is weakened if institutional protection consistently predates
sustained reform print, if print-network measures do not distinguish adopting
from non-adopting jurisdictions, or if comparable organization appears through
oral, manuscript, and clerical networks without the proposed print paths.

### State-protection account

The state-protection account proposes that princes and magistrates created the
safe jurisdiction, appointments, enforcement, and material transfers that made
public reform durable. Print amplified a choice whose decisive cause was
political authority. It expects protected printers and preachers to persist
where unprotected ones disappear, ordinances and enforcement capacity to
separate public institutions from private dissent, and changes in alliance or
war to redirect print control.

This account is weakened if similar protection fails where the print network is
thin, if political adoption follows rather than leads documented mass
circulation and local mobilization, or if reform institutions persist after
their protectors withdraw without replacement.

### Demand-and-persuasion account

The demand-and-persuasion account proposes that theological argument,
anticlerical grievance, devotion, preaching, images, song, and face-to-face
persuasion generated audiences that printers then served. It expects content,
genre, language, and trusted local intermediaries to predict reception better
than press access alone. The relevant communication process is mixed-media,
not a text moving unaided from producer to reader.

This account is weakened if similar messages and grievances produce sharply
different outcomes that align with network access, or if reprint connections
predict diffusion after differences in content, language, and local
intermediation are addressed.

### Print-market account

The print-market account proposes that printers pursued saleable controversy,
while reformers provided short, repeatable products and new readers. Causal
influence therefore runs in both directions: religious conflict changes the
industry as the industry changes the reach and tempo of conflict. It expects
output to respond to demand, reprints to cluster around commercially successful
forms, and firms to alter topics or conceal identifiers when legal risk changes.

This account is weakened if publication patterns do not respond to price,
format, demand, or enforcement, or if noncommercial patronage explains the
supposed market response.

### Coupled-threshold account

The project's strongest provisional account is a threshold rather than a
single-priority explanation. Cheap repeatable messages increase exposure;
preaching, discussion, and correspondence translate exposure into local ties;
protection lowers the risk of open coordination; ordinances and personnel turn
coordination into institutions; and those institutions sponsor, standardize,
or suppress further communication. The expected signature is ordered feedback:
no domain is sufficient, but removing a consequential link makes the divergent
paths harder to explain.

This account risks explaining every sequence after the fact. Before evidence is
coded, the reduced baselines are a topology-plus-demand account without
political protection, a state-plus-grievance account without measured print
relations, and an independent-shocks account. The five-domain reconstruction
earns its complexity only if it identifies timing, geographic variation, or a
failed transition that at least one reduced account misclassifies.

## Units, periods, and observations

The broad spatial units are political territories and self-governing cities in
the German-speaking Holy Roman Empire and states and governed cities on the
Italian peninsula. Electoral Saxony and the Republic of Venice receive nested
process traces. Historical boundaries, jurisdictional changes, and the
distinction between a printing place and its governing territory must be
preserved rather than projected onto modern states.

The observation window has four phases. The 1470–1516 baseline measures the
precontroversy print industry, political jurisdictions, reform currents, and
church institutions. The 1517–1525 phase follows initial controversy,
publication, diffusion, repression, and popular mobilization. The 1526–1546
phase follows territorial selection, church orders, dissent networks, and
institution building. The 1547–1565 phase follows war, settlement,
inquisitorial enforcement, indexes, Trent, migration, and confessional
consolidation. Dates remain event-level inside these intervals; the phases are
not substitutes for chronology.

The minimum event record contains an earliest and latest plausible date,
place, jurisdiction, actor, event type, source, source standpoint, confidence,
and links to preceding and following events. Uncertainty must remain an
interval. A publication date, arrival date, reading date, ordinance date, and
implementation date are different observations.

## Communication layers and measures

The design separates substrate capacity from institutions and use. The
physical-production layer records presses, paper supply when recoverable, and
printing places. The publishing layer records printers, publishers, authors,
formats, languages, topics, editions, and declared or concealed places. The
reproduction layer links editions that reprint, translate, abridge, answer, or
imitate an earlier work. The distribution and reception layer records shipment,
sale, possession, reading, preaching, correspondence, and citation only where a
source documents it. The control layer records privileges, licensing,
prohibition, confiscation, searches, trials, and punishment.

An edition is the default bibliographic unit; a surviving copy is not a new
edition, and an edition is not a known print run or readership count. Network
nodes may be printing places, firms, authors, intermediaries, or jurisdictions,
but each graph must use one declared node rule. An edge must state whether it
means shared text, a business relation, documented transport, or reception.

The initial measures are annual editions by place, language, format, and coded
confessional position; entry and exit of firms; time to first reprint;
geographic distance and jurisdictional crossings between editions; number and
diversity of reprinting centers; concentration of output by place and firm;
and the delay from first local exposure to organized dissent, countermeasure,
or public ordinance. Degree, betweenness, modularity, or diffusion speed will
be calculated only for a layer whose edges make the measure historically
meaningful.

Catalog presence measures survival and description as well as production.
Missing, destroyed, anonymous, falsely imprinted, clandestine, and uncataloged
works are expected to be nonrandom. Estimates must therefore report coverage,
deduplicate records across catalogs, retain uncertain attributions, and test
whether conclusions survive exclusion of records known only through later
references.

## Five-domain reconstruction protocol

Each case will be narrated five times from the same dated event ledger. This is
an ablation exercise, not a license to invent five independent stories.

The topology-first narrative begins with changes in production, reproduction,
distribution, reception, and control relations. It asks whether these changes
explain when and where collective religious action became feasible, then traces
effects into production, artifacts, worldview, and political organization.

The production-first narrative begins with firms, patronage, urban markets,
paper and labor, church resources, and the material interests of rulers and
printers. It asks whether demand and resource organization selected print uses
rather than being reorganized by them.

The artifact-first narrative begins with pamphlets, Bibles, catechisms, images,
songs, sermons, ordinances, and indexes as reproducible forms. It asks what
their size, language, genre, design, portability, and reusability allowed actors
to do across print, oral, visual, and ritual settings.

The worldview-first narrative begins with theology, devotion, anticlericalism,
humanism, eschatology, and concepts of authority. It asks whether ideas created
the demand, alliances, and classifications that later reorganized media and
institutions.

The political-organization-first narrative begins with imperial, princely,
municipal, papal, Venetian, and inquisitorial capacities. It asks how
jurisdiction, protection, war, diplomacy, licensing, and coercion opened or
closed channels and converted communication into public rules.

After the five narratives are completed, the comparison will score only
declared discriminating questions: which account best explains the onset of
rapid diffusion, the geography and timing of public ordinances, the survival of
organized dissent without an ordinance, the timing and effects of control, and
the divergence between Saxony and Venice. “everything affected everything” is
not a successful result.

## Evidence plan

The bibliographic backbone will combine the
[VD 16](https://www.bsb-muenchen.de/kompetenzzentren-und-landesweite-dienste/kompetenzzentren/vd-16/),
the German national bibliography for the sixteenth-century German language
area; [EDIT16](https://edit16.iccu.sbn.it/en/), the national census of editions
printed in sixteenth-century Italy and Italian-language editions printed
elsewhere; and the [Universal Short Title
Catalogue](https://www.ustc.ac.uk/) for cross-regional reconciliation. Their
scopes and cataloging practices differ, so raw result counts will not be
treated as comparable output. The first data release must include query dates,
field mappings, deduplication rules, and manual validation samples.

German institutional dates will begin from territorial and municipal church
ordinances, including the source series described by Jeremiah Dittmar and Ralf
Meisenzahl, and will be cross-checked against local scholarship and dated legal
texts. Davide Cantoni's territory and city chronology is a reusable comparison,
not an unquestioned ground truth. Italian events will begin from state and
church prohibitions, trial and visitation records where editions make them
available, correspondence, and histories based on the archives of the Roman
and Venetian Inquisitions. Absence of a Protestant ordinance is not evidence
that no dissent existed.

Primary printed artifacts will be sampled from the same bibliographic strata,
with surviving copies inspected when digitized. Coding will record title-page
identification, language, format, paratext, images, named audience, calls to
action, and evidence of reuse in preaching or regulation. The sample must
include Catholic answers and ordinary devotional print, not only works later
canonized as Protestant.

Oral communication, ritual, images, manuscript, and face-to-face organization
are required evidence categories. Print exposure will not be inferred from
literacy alone, and nonreaders will not be classified as unexposed when a
printed text was preached, read aloud, sung, pictured, or discussed.

## Source criticism and known threats

Survival bias favors works collected by institutions and later confessional
traditions. Censorship can both destroy evidence and generate unusually rich
trial records. Inquisitorial testimony was produced under coercive conditions,
while Protestant and Catholic chronicles had reasons to magnify success,
persecution, unity, or deviance. Every evidence record must identify the source
producer, purpose, proximity to the event, and what the source could not
observe.

Confessional coding is unstable before later boundaries hardened. “evangelical,”
“Lutheran,” “heretical,” “spiritual,” and “Catholic reform” cannot be collapsed
into a binary by borrowing an inquisitor's or later historian's category.
Ambiguous items will retain multiple candidate codes and the rule used in each
sensitivity analysis.

Political adoption is endogenous. Rulers could protect reform for theological,
fiscal, dynastic, constitutional, or military reasons, and those choices could
also attract printers and authors. Event order, negative cases inside each
region, pre-1517 print baselines, and changes around protection or repression
will be used to probe direction. Distance to Wittenberg is not automatically a
communication measure because it can proxy geography, political neighborhood,
or strategic imitation.

The two catalogs do not observe the same universe, and Italy and the Empire do
not offer symmetrical institutional records. The analysis will prioritize
within-region contrasts before comparing standardized summaries across regions.
It will not manufacture symmetry by treating a missing Italian ordinance as
equivalent to a dated German non-adoption.

## Decision rules and possible conclusions

CM-07 receives support if measured communication relations precede and
distinguish diffusion or organization within both regions after plausible
rivals are traced, even when they do not produce the same institution. It is
weakened if topology adds no discriminating information beyond demand,
political protection, or ordinary geographic access.

CM-08 receives support only if communication change explains consequential
timing or variation that a state-first or production-first sequence misses. It
is revised toward reciprocal causation if protection and print repeatedly
generate one another without a defensible prior step. It is rejected for this
transition if political or productive organization consistently selects the
relevant print network before that network constrains institutional change.

CM-09 receives support if the five-domain reconstruction correctly identifies
a divergence or failed transition that the reduced narratives misclassify. It
is weakened if the domains duplicate variables, chronology cannot distinguish
couplings, or a smaller account explains the same observations.

A plausible negative result is theoretically useful: print may accelerate and
reshape controversy while remaining neither a sufficient cause nor the
causally prior constraint on public reorganization. Another is that the same
substrate expands both dissident reach and the state's capacity to classify,
standardize, and suppress. Either result would require replacing the
manifesto's singular language of causal priority with a conditional account of
communication inside an institutionally selected circuit.

## Research sequence and stopping point

The first pass will freeze a codebook and jurisdiction list, build the dated
institutional outcome ledger, and draw a stratified bibliographic sample for
the four phases. Process traces for Saxony and Venice will then identify the
actors and events needed to interpret the aggregate patterns. Only after that
pass will the project decide whether the catalogs support a reproducible
network dataset or only bounded descriptive comparisons.

The case is not ready for an essay when a graph can be drawn. It is ready when
the German result survives at least one within-Empire negative comparison, the
Italian result distinguishes public non-adoption from absent dissent, each
causal link has a dated source or is marked speculative, and at least one rival
account has been made stronger rather than mentioned ceremonially.

## Conclusions and Next Steps

This design warrants one conclusion now: print topology cannot be tested as a
singular prior cause by comparing aggregate printing and confessional outcomes.
A defensible test must separate communication reach from product demand,
political protection, market organization, censorship, and ordinary geographic
access, then compare their timing within each region before comparing Germany
with Italy. The paired design therefore turns CM-07 through CM-09 into
discriminating questions, but supplies no evidence yet for raising or lowering
their confidence.

The immediate next step is to freeze the jurisdiction list, outcome codebook,
phase boundaries, and missing-data rules before collecting observations. The
first evidence pass should then build the dated ordinance and censorship ledger
and two process traces, Saxony and Venice. Its decision point is whether the
available catalogs support a reproducible network dataset or only bounded
descriptive comparisons. Either result is useful: the latter would redirect
the case toward source-grounded sequence analysis instead of manufacturing a
network measure from incompatible records.
