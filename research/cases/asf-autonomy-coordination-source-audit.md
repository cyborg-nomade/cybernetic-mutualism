[ASF preregistration](asf-autonomy-coordination-preregistration.md), not an
evidence pass. Sources below establish candidate access routes and current

## Incidental Outcome Exposure

The calendar search excerpt exposed selected 2025–2026 resolutions concerning
project creation, retirement, chair changes, and foundation offices. Among
them were 2025 retirement entries for Olingo, Traffic Control, Kibble,
Portals, and Griffin, plus a January 2026 Lucene chair-change entry and other
out-of-cohort changes. These were returned by the search engine while checking
archive availability, not sought as candidate explanations. The fixed cohort
is HTTP Server, Tomcat, Maven, and Ant; none of those named outcome snippets
is being used to select a favorable conflict or code a causal outcome.

The registration nevertheless does **not** claim complete outcome blindness.
The analyst also has general prior familiarity with these software projects;
this is a convenience-based case choice. Future evidence reports must retain
this exposure disclosure, including if an incidental foundation-wide event
later proves relevant to the selected relationships. Do not erase exposure by
calling the retrospective study a prospective experiment.

## Access Risks and Permitted First Checks

After acceptance, check the public calendar/minutes and each fixed project's
documented development-list archive route, using only publicly accessible
records. The exact historical mailing-list aliases and redirects must be
recorded in the retrieval manifest rather than guessed from project names.
Record errors, redactions, and coverage month by month. Retry ordinary public
access paths or documented public mirrors when necessary, de-duplicating
originals. Do not treat search snippets as replacements for missing records.

Verify historical PMC eligibility and governing rules before interpreting
authority. The present-day committee index and guide are not evidence of the
2023 rule set. No private schedule or correspondence is necessary to run the
access audit; if the causal comparison needs unavailable private material, its
identification gate fails. A failed gate is a registered result, not permission
to change the project sample or infer hidden exchanges.

Archive completeness, the number of eligible opportunities, sufficient
variation, and the availability of a second human reviewer remain unverified.
The packet is a frozen design with explicit feasibility gates, not a promise
that the public records will meet them. No interviews, external contact,
account access, or personal-data enrichment is authorized here.

## Conclusions and Next Steps

The inspected sources support a candidate public-record study and identify
known confidentiality constraints. They do not warrant any directed-effect
claim, historical policy attribution, or assertion that the cohort has passed
the access gate. CM-01 and CM-04 gain no empirical support from this audit.

Preserve this disclosure with the registered protocol. After review and
separate authorization for the evidence pass, the next discriminating action
is the fixed-cohort source and eligibility audit, not selection of illustrative
success stories.
