## Saxony: Authorization, Text, and Territorial Selection

The electoral sequence connects authorized inspection to a reproducible teaching
artifact. E03 records the 1527 commission; E04 records the 1528 instruction
addressed to pastors. Heidelberg's source description explicitly connects the
text to visitation experience. This is evidence of institutional work producing
and targeting communication, rather than a sequence in which the printed instruction
first creates the inspection machinery. It does not settle what earlier pamphlet
circulation contributed to the political choice. E02 supplies a contemporaneous
control attempt and reported dissemination, but no measured local exposure series.

The ducal comparison makes political boundaries consequential. E05 records the
Freiberg exception before Henry's accession; E06 records wider implementation
after succession in 1539. An analysis that labels the entire duchy uniformly
unreformed until that year would erase the very internal variation useful for
tracing protection. E07 then links administrative reuse to continuation. These
observations strengthen the state-protection account for the timing of expansion.
They do not isolate protection from theological commitments, imported personnel,
or material resources, and they do not establish equal print exposure in ES/DS.

The process trace's important positive link is therefore **authority and personnel
→ production and targeting of instruction**. Local implementation is reported
only indirectly; comprehension, circulation volume, and uniform compliance are
not established. Feedback through standardization remains untested. The change
in electoral geography after 1547 must also remain visible. E19 establishes an
imperial legal settlement but
cannot repair missing parish returns or turn the former electoral territory into
an unchanged statistical unit.

## Venice: Dissent, Selective Control, and a Chronological Check

E01 establishes a baseline liturgical-print candidate. E08–E10 distinguish
organized correspondence, publication, manuscript advocacy and hopes for public
preaching. The evidence is incompatible with treating the difficult case as simply
unexposed or devoid of organized dissent. The cited correspondents, however, are
interested advocates. Their expectations are not public authorization. E18 supplies
affirmative evidence of Catholic institutional adaptation; the present ledger does
not license a blanket zero for every locality in the Republic.

E11 records a mixed institutional arrangement rather than the arrival of control
into a previously institutionless market. In S13/S14, the lay magistrates also
protect Venetian jurisdiction against ecclesiastical encroachment. S11 offers
perceived subversion as a mechanism for selective repression. These distinctions
matter because “the state” is not one actor with one stable censorship preference.

E13–E17 show why the dated ledger must preserve steps: ordering and printing a
list do not establish its application. E14 supplies opposition and non-application;
E15 supplies a distinct coercive episode; subsequent indexes differ in enforcement.
One can investigate institutional selection of communication without inferring a
single switch that uniformly shuts down an entire industry. The Jewish printing
episode remains a separate affected sector, not a proxy for Protestant adoption.

The most discriminating chronology check concerns S12's §3.1 narrative linking
Venice's adjustment to the year's European military/political changes. S13 and
S14 date E11 to **22 April 1547**; S15 dates E12 to **24 April 1547**. On these
reported dates, knowledge of the battle's result cannot cause the earlier act.
This rejects that specific direct-trigger reading, not the broader wartime,
diplomatic, or anticipatory-pressure explanation. Original decree dating and
preceding negotiations are the next checks. A year-level before/after variable
would hide this problem. It also follows that this timing check does **not**
independently refute S12's later firm-level censorship estimates, which are not
replicated here.

Labels concern the explanatory reach of this evidence, not model fit statistics.
`compatible` means the observations can be accommodated without a demonstrated
advantage; `favours` means a specific sequence strengthens an account while rivals
remain; `unresolved` means the discriminating observation is missing. A contradiction
of an auxiliary direct-trigger reading is not counted as a victory for all five
domains.

| Saxony/Venice divergence; E03–E19 | unresolved | compatible and strengthened by institutional steps | unresolved | Full outcome durability, comparable topology, and production measures remain incomplete. No reduced account is demonstrably outperformed by all five domains. |

Two tempting shortcuts are rejected. First, the low-complexity baseline is not
“printing alone causes Protestantism”: the frozen topology-plus-demand account
already contains demand. Second, a state account can include bargaining and
selective enforcement; finding those processes does not by itself defeat it.
The five-domain account must earn its additional variables against those stronger
alternatives. This pass identifies no such earned increment.

The catalog audit selects bounded process tracing, not network construction, for

aggregate production comparisons. The stop decision follows the freeze's access
and missing-data rules, and makes no claim that catalog access or network research
is impossible. The BSB object audit already demonstrates a usable technical route;
what is missing is a bounded comparable universe and defensible edges.

The most economical next historical work is a small discriminating packet:
the original 22 April act and preceding negotiations; Saxon visitation returns
linked to the printed instruction; and dated Venetian evidence separating
authorized public worship from dissent or tolerated exceptions. Each has a named
decision to change. Additional famous pamphlets without those links have lower
priority. Catalog exploration should first test the existing S12 supplement and
a small edition crosswalk before committing to a larger extraction.

## Conclusions and Next Steps

This first pass warrants institutional selection as an explicit working account,
separates dissent from public authorization, and rejects a particular reversal of
cause and effect in the 1547 chronology. It turns a broad comparison into auditable
records and identifies the observations required to improve it. It does not yet
warrant causal priority for topology, a completed durability comparison, or a
confidence upgrade for CM-07–CM-09.

The second cycle now has a reviewable item-5 evidence packet. Its next workflow
action is review; after acceptance, the cycle-close decision record can apply all
five completion criteria and prioritize the discriminating follow-up above against
the separately preregistered ASF collection. No blog essay or manifesto revision
is implied by this first pass.
