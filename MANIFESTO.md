# cybernetic mutualism

## a provisional manifesto

this is not a catechism and it is not a prediction delivered from the end of
history. it is the declaration of a research program.

cybernetic mutualism begins from an old intuition that mutualism itself never
quite finished developing: society moves through relations whose opposed terms
cannot be eliminated without destroying the relation. liberty and authority,
competition and cooperation, individual and collective force, centralisation
and decentralisation do not await reconciliation in a final order. they produce
one another. they limit one another. they intensify one another. their conflict
is not noise laid over society; it is one of the processes by which society is
made.

the name replaces an earlier one, *dark mutualism*. the darkness named a break
with optimistic humanism, linear progress, and the belief that correct ideals
could simply be chosen and installed. the cybernetic name is more exact. the
object is not darkness but feedback; not transgression but determination; not a
new political identity but a model of how social orders reproduce and transform
themselves.

## 1. mutualism is a theory of progress

mutualism is usually remembered as a collection of economic proposals: mutual
credit, possession rather than dominion, federation, reciprocal exchange. those
proposals matter, but beneath them lies a more general concern with progress:
serialized transformation driven by collective force and contradiction.

this progress is neither a straight line nor a moral ascent. history moves in
waves, spirals, captures, releases, and phase transitions. a decentralising
innovation may enable a new central power. a central apparatus may create the
infrastructure from which decentralisation later erupts. regression at one scale
may be adaptation at another. no local victory abolishes the process that made
it possible.

## 2. antinomies do not end

the central Proudhonian inheritance is the indissolubility of antinomies. an
opposition is not necessarily resolved by selecting one pole or by lifting both
into a harmonious synthesis. opposed forces can remain necessary, mutually
contained, and dynamically unstable.

this is the conceptual bridge to cybernetics. recurrent excitation, inhibition,
delay, saturation, adaptation, and error correction give formal languages to
what Proudhon described philosophically. equilibrium, when it exists, is an
activity rather than a destination. stability is reproduced, not possessed.

there is no reason to assume a final decentralisation, final central plan,
final class, final market, or final revolution. durable orders can generate new
asymmetries and counterforces when positions accumulate advantages,
environments change, or prior controls provoke organized responses. this is a
historical hypothesis, not a universal law: some orders may remove a specified
asymmetry without replacing it, and external shocks may explain recurrence
better. the disappearance of one contradiction can change the field in which
others develop.

## 3. communication is material

human societies exist only where communication can be reproduced. speech,
writing, archives, roads, printing, broadcasting, computation, protocols, and
platforms are not passive vessels carrying an independently formed society.
they constrain who can address whom, across what distance, with what durability,
latency, fidelity, cost, and capacity for reply.

the primary object of cybernetic mutualism is therefore the topology of
communication: the weighted and directed networks through which signals,
commands, models, promises, prices, threats, and stories circulate.

this is a materialist claim. a communication substrate defines a space of
feasible social connections before any particular institution occupies that
space. it does not mechanically determine one outcome, but it makes some forms
cheap, others costly, and others impossible. topology is not the only cause in
history. it is proposed as a causally prior constraint that must itself be
tested against production, ecology, demography, war, and accident.

## 4. society is a recursive circuit

a first approximation of the social circuit is:

> communication topology → productive organisation → material and cultural
> artefacts → worldview and philosophy → political organisation → communication
> topology

call these domains `T`, `P`, `A`, `W`, and `S`.

the arrows are not one-way. every domain can act upon every other, at different
speeds and scales. political institutions build networks; productive systems
fund media; cultural artefacts reorganise perception; worldviews alter
institutions; communication technologies emerge from prior productive and
scientific arrangements. shocks may enter anywhere.

the claim is not that everything is feedback. the claim is that most durable
human phenomena eventually enter this circuit, and that their effects cannot be
understood in isolation from it.

## 5. substrates define spaces; phases trace paths

historical "stages" should not be identified with a fixed degree of
centralisation. a stage is better understood as a dominant communication
substrate defining a feasible topology space, `Ω`. centralising and
decentralising phases are trajectories within that space.

a provisional sequence of ideal types is:

1. oral and embodied communication;
2. durable scribal writing and archives;
3. mechanical print and distributed copying;
4. broadcast media with scarce return channels;
5. digital networks and inexpensive many-to-many communication;
6. platform capture and the recentralised internet;
7. possible forms of persistent, global, live interconnection.

the sequence is neither universal in every detail nor irreversible. substrates
overlap. older forms survive inside newer ones. different regions occupy
different phases at once. the useful claim is narrower: each substrate changes
the available graph structures, and those changes apply selection pressure to
institutions built for the preceding space.

## 6. centralisation and decentralisation mutually produce one another

decentralisation creates variation, local knowledge, experimentation, and new
edges. it can also create fragmentation, coordination costs, and vulnerability
to actors capable of aggregation. centralisation creates standards, memory,
scale, and coordinated power. it can also suppress variation, distort signals,
accumulate maintenance costs, and become incapable of correcting its own
models.

neither pole is intrinsically emancipatory. networks advertised as distributed
can conceal concentrated control; central infrastructures can enable autonomous
activity at their edges. the task is to describe centralisation by measurable
relations—degree distributions, control of routing, betweenness, ownership,
modularity, protocol authority—not by institutional labels.

the expected historical pattern is recurrent: decentralising innovations open
a topology space; successful nodes accumulate links; standards and hubs capture
the new flows; the captured system becomes a fetter; pressure accumulates for a
new substrate or a new arrangement. this is a hypothesis about coupled dynamics,
not a law exempt from evidence.

## 7. conflict can be productive without being good

mutual excitation is not the same as harmony. rivals alter one another's
behaviour and capacities. competition can stimulate invention, discipline, and
adaptation. it can also create arms races, monopoly, exhaustion, and collapse.

productive rivalry requires countervailing processes: resource limits,
saturation, transparency, reciprocal guarantees, exit, federation, punishment
of capture, and mechanisms that preserve variation. negative feedback does not
abolish positive feedback; it keeps excitation within a viable range.

this is an engineering description, not a moral justification of suffering.
the fact that domination recurs does not make domination right. status
differences may be difficult to eliminate, while every durable power still
seeks legitimacy. one of political philosophy's permanent problems is that
social dominance is recurrent but cannot derive moral authority from recurrence
alone. institutions can at most make power constrained, reversible, legible,
and contestable.

## 8. marx is necessary and insufficient

marx supplies the strongest account of capital as an impersonal social process,
of exploitation and class formation, and of historical orders producing the
conditions of their own transformation. cybernetic mutualism accepts class
struggle as a central motor of history while refusing to treat the relations of
production as the deepest available layer in every case.

communication topology conditions which productive relations can be
coordinated, observed, enforced, and imagined. the base is therefore widened—or
displaced—from production alone to the material organisation of communication.

the marxian inheritance must also be stripped of any guaranteed terminus.
capitalism does not automatically deliver communism, and socialism does not end
class formation. anti-capitalist institutions can intensify accumulation;
capitalist mechanisms can be deployed by revolutionary states; new elites arise
inside systems created to abolish them. china's simultaneous political
centralisation, decentralised experimentation, and accelerated capital
accumulation is not an embarrassment to be explained away. it is a primary test
case.

lenin and mao matter because their strategies helped produce and sustain the
largest revolutionary processes of the modern period. they are neither saints
nor detachable manuals. their successes, errors, information failures, and
institutional afterlives are evidence. dengist reform and brezhnevian stagnation
offer contrasting problems of adaptation, model correction, and organisational
rigidity.

## 9. direction does not require a destination

cybernetic mutualism rejects an end of history, but not every historical
direction. the long tendency proposed here is toward communication systems of
greater extent, persistence, speed, differentiation, and complexity.

complexity is not moral progress. a more complex system can be more oppressive,
fragile, or wasteful. nor is complexity guaranteed to increase forever: energy
loss, ecological limits, war, and catabolic decline can destroy the material
basis of coordination. direction is a statistical tendency operating through
selection and constraint, not providence.

luhmann's account of society as self-reproducing communication helps remove the
human subject from the centre of this story. cliodynamics, evolutionary theory,
world-systems analysis, institutional economics, and complexity science offer
neighbouring models. none should be imported as ornament. each must change the
theory's variables or predictions to earn its place.

## 10. no free will is required

the model does not depend on uncaused individual or collective choice. agents
act through bounded models, inherited drives, material incentives, institutions,
and signals. what consciousness experiences as decision is part of the causal
machinery, not an exception to it.

this does not make modelling, criticism, or political action pointless. a model
entering a society becomes another cause inside the circuit. description can
alter expectation; expectation can alter behaviour; behaviour can alter the
system described. second-order effects are part of the object.

determinism is not certainty. limited information, nonlinear dynamics, noise,
and sensitivity to initial conditions make exact prediction impossible even in
a determined system. the appropriate ambition is conditional explanation:
given these constraints and parameters, which regimes become more likely?

## 11. politics is a problem of control without a controller

society cannot be steered as a simple machine from a position outside society.
states, parties, markets, sciences, and media are themselves partial systems
with delayed, distorted, and strategically manipulated information.

political organisation should therefore be analysed in terms of sensing,
memory, response time, requisite variety, error correction, and capture. plans
fail when their controllers cannot represent the environments they act upon.
markets fail when their signals reward costs they refuse to register. democracy
fails when opinion channels can be monopolised. bureaucracy fails when metrics
replace the purposes they were meant to indicate.

the practical question is not "central plan or free market?" it is which
combination of distributed experimentation, shared infrastructure, enforceable
constraints, and recursive audit remains viable under specified conditions.

## 12. the theory must risk being wrong

cybernetic mutualism aims to be both a general theory and a diagnostic toolkit.
that ambition is empty unless its concepts become operational.

the research program must therefore:

- represent communication systems as multilayer weighted directed graphs;
- define centralisation, decentralisation, control, entropy, and adaptation at
  explicit scales;
- distinguish communication substrate from the institutions using it;
- model delays, saturation, resource gradients, and cross-domain coupling;
- compare continuous dynamical models with agent-based microfoundations;
- reconstruct historical transitions with data available independently of the
  theory;
- publish negative results and competing causal explanations.

the priority of communication topology would be weakened if changes in
production or political organisation consistently precede and predict topology
better than topology predicts them. the stage model would fail if its supposed
substrate constraints do not constrain observed network forms. oscillatory
models would fail if their cycles disappear under plausible measurement or are
better explained by uncoupled external shocks. a theory protected from these
possibilities would be mythology with equations.

## 13. this manifesto is an invitation to correction

cybernetic mutualism is not yet a finished doctrine. its inherited sources must
be verified. its historical narratives must be separated from suggestive
analogies. its mathematical sketches must become real models. its models must
meet evidence. its political intuitions must not be smuggled into descriptive
variables as foregone conclusions.

the project remains mutualist because it expects development through encounter:
between opposed forces, between disciplines, between formal models and
historical cases, between a theory and the evidence that resists it.

there will be no final synthesis. there can be better feedback.
